Anomalies

    Anomalies

    What is shown. 1° regions whose activity this month is far from the same calendar month in the other years of the archive. Counts are adjusted for the satellites flying each year, so a new satellite does not look like a fire.

    The score. A robust z: the distance of log(1 + cell-days) from the median of the other years, in units of their median absolute deviation. Regions are shown from ±3, with at least 8 years to compare and 5 cell-days of activity. One extreme year does not hide another, and a doubling counts alike in quiet and busy places.

    Colours. The difference from the median, on the same scale as History: orange above, blue below.

    Limits. A month needs two satellites that observed every day of it, so the month in progress and the Terra-only months of 2000–2002 are not tested. Cloud and smoke hide fire, so a low month can also be a cloudy one.

    What the numbers mean

    Active cell-day. The Earth is divided into cells 0.1° across, about 11 km. A cell counts once for each day a satellite saw fire in it, however many detections it held. So 300 cell-days can be 300 places burning for a day, or 10 places burning for a month.

    Why not count detections? VIIRS sees several times more hotspots than MODIS in the same fire, and the number of satellites has changed since 2000. Counting cells that burned, not pixels that fired, narrows that gap. Where the sensor calibration has been run, the rest is adjusted from the years the satellites overlap.

    Comparison. The percentage sets this month against the same calendar month in earlier years. For a month still in progress it compares the same days of the month, seen by the same satellites.

    What is left out. Low-confidence detections, and hotspots NASA classifies as volcanoes, gas flares, other static sources or offshore.
